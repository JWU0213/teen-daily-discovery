CENTRAL AFRICA · PUBLIC HEALTH · SCIENCE

Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 11,000 cases as vaccine research moves forward

WHAT HAPPENED

The DRC in Central Africa reported 11,042 Ebola infections and 5,387 deaths, AP said on November 11. WHO says cases appeared in five countries. Kenya discharged its final patient today and starts a 42-day countdown; the DRC still has cases in eight provinces.

WHY IT MATTERS

This outbreak is caused by Bundibugyo ebolavirus. It spreads mainly through direct contact with body fluids of someone sick or deceased, or contaminated objects. Safe care and trusted information matter.

THE HARD PART

Conflict, poor roads, crowded clinics and rumours make it harder to reach patients and earn trust.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Teams trace contacts and support clinics. WHO recommended two candidate vaccines for a Bundibugyo trial on November 4; national approval is still needed. Results are expected in early 2027.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

No vaccine or specific treatment is approved for this virus type yet. Trials take time, and vaccines cannot replace safe clinics, trained staff and community trust.

How could a trusted local message help people stay safe without spreading fear?

TÜRKIYE · CLIMATE · INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION

World leaders meet at COP31: promises now need practical plans

WHAT HAPPENED

COP31 runs November 9–20 in Antalya, Türkiye. Nearly 200 countries are negotiating; Australia leads formal talks while Türkiye hosts. The UN chief says progress is too slow.

WHY IT MATTERS

Climate change affects heat, storms, food, water and homes. Electric transport and buildings can cut fossil-fuel use if their power is cleaner.

THE HARD PART

Countries need reliable, affordable power but have different budgets. A proposed 35% electricity-use target for 2035 is voluntary and does not require clean power.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Negotiators discuss clean energy, stronger grids and climate adaptation. The real test is whether plans receive funding and get built.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

More electricity can help, but coal power still pollutes. New grids and batteries cost money; workers and household bills matter too.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

What should a climate promise include: cleaner power, affordable bills, reliable supply—or all three?

HOUSEHOLD BILLS · SOLAR POWER · JOBS

Five countries back a plan to double the number of homes with rooftop solar

WHAT HAPPENED

Five countries—Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu—backed a voluntary plan to double rooftop-solar homes from 150 million to 300 million by 2030.

WHY IT MATTERS

Panels make electricity at home; families may buy less from the grid. Batteries can store power for night-time or emergencies.

THE HARD PART

Panels cost money upfront. Homes need suitable roofs, safe wiring and trained installers; grids may need upgrades.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Supporters want clear rules, financial help, installer training and better batteries. The declaration is a goal, not a law.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

Solar can lower bills and pollution, but equipment needs materials and later repair or recycling. Savings depend on local conditions.

🤖 Technology & Science

One research team is using AI to help check software with mathematics. Another has solved a mystery about a star that keeps flashing.

AI · DIGITAL PRIVACY · CYBERSECURITY

AI agents help researchers mathematically check part of a messaging system's security

WHAT HAPPENED

Google DeepMind says AlphaProof Code worked with human experts and AI agents to prove selected security properties of libsignal, code behind the Signal Protocol. Lean checked the proofs.

WHY IT MATTERS

Encryption scrambles messages so outsiders cannot easily read them. Mathematical checks may help catch mistakes in privacy software.

THE HARD PART

AI can make mistakes. A proof only covers the exact rules researchers wrote; it cannot prove they asked every important question.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Humans wrote and reviewed the rules. AI agents generated millions of proof-code lines; Lean checked them. The system also caught two old bugs in a test version.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

The study covered selected reference-code properties, not the whole Signal app or group messaging. It is not proof that any app is unbreakable.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

What evidence would you want before trusting AI to check software protecting your private messages?

SPACE · STARS · NASA CHANDRA

What keeps making this giant star flash every 113 days?

WHAT HAPPENED

NASA's Chandra telescope helped explain repeated flashes from AT 2016blu, a giant star in galaxy NGC 4559. A hidden companion pulls gas from it every 113 days.

WHY IT MATTERS

The gas heats up and shines in X-rays, helping scientists understand stars that flare dramatically but survive instead of exploding.

THE HARD PART

The companion could be a black hole or a neutron star. Astronomers have not confirmed which.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Researchers combined 15 years of visible-light observations with a Chandra X-ray observation during an outburst in March 2026.

🎨 Creativity & Inspiration

Three high-school researchers show how simple materials and repeated experiments can lead to an engineering idea.

Illustrative photo of a curved gridshell structure; this is not the students' prototype. Photo: Jeanpei, CC BY-SA 4.0. Image details and licence ↗
UNITED STATES · YOUNG ENGINEERS · DESIGN

Three students turn wooden sticks, hot water and 3D-printed parts into curved structures

WHAT HAPPENED

Three high-school students at Hofstra—Madison Lee, Taylor Yeh and Joyce Jia Xu—made curved structures using wood dowels, 3D-printed connectors and hot water. Their work reached an engineering conference in Turin.

WHY IT MATTERS

Curved gridshells can be strong while using materials efficiently. Low-cost models let designers test shapes first.

THE HARD PART

Hot water softens the PLA connectors so a flat grid bends. A small model does not prove a full-size roof is safe.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

The students designed connectors, used computer-aided design and improved prototypes through repeated tests. They were lead designers and first authors.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

Cheap materials invite experimentation, but plastic needs responsible handling. Real buildings require strength, fire and weather tests.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

Could you bend paper strips into a strong curve? What would you change if it collapsed?

🐾 Nature & Planet

A wildlife disease is putting pressure on animals that gather in large breeding colonies far from most people.

Southern elephant seal pups at Gold Harbour, South Georgia. This is a wildlife photograph, not a picture of sick animals. Photo: Anne Dirkse, CC BY-SA 4.0. Image details and licence ↗
SOUTH ATLANTIC · WILDLIFE · BIRD FLU

Bird flu is putting southern elephant seals at greater risk

WHAT HAPPENED

H5N1 has reached four of five main southern elephant seal populations. The Guardian reports over 53,000 female seals died on South Georgia by 2025; some colonies lost over 90% of pups. A study found 47% fewer breeding females at three colonies in 2024 than in 2022.

WHY IT MATTERS

South Georgia is a remote sub-Antarctic island in the South Atlantic. The IUCN moved the species to Vulnerable in April 2026 because adult losses threaten recovery.

THE HARD PART

The 47% figure comes from three colonies. Counting every seal is difficult, and not every missing animal is confirmed dead.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Researchers monitor colonies. BirdLife International is reassessing four affected birds, including great skuas and Humboldt penguins.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

Field teams must avoid disturbing wildlife or spreading disease. Better counts help conservation groups decide where limited resources matter most.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

How could scientists monitor a remote colony without disturbing it?
