TÜRKIYE · CLIMATE · WORLD COOPERATION

Nearly 200 countries meet to turn climate promises into action

WHAT HAPPENED

On November 9, the COP31 United Nations climate conference opened in Antalya, a city on Türkiye’s Mediterranean coast. Representatives from nearly 200 countries are meeting until November 20. They will discuss how to reduce planet-warming pollution, prepare cities for extreme weather and help countries pay for changes such as cleaner electricity systems.

WHY IT MATTERS / WHY IT HAPPENED

Burning coal, oil and gas releases greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. The effects include hotter heatwaves and heavier rainfall in some places. Scientists say the world is on track to pass the Paris Agreement’s goal of limiting long-term warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. That does not mean every place warms by exactly 1.5 degrees, or that action is pointless. It means the risks grow as warming increases, so every fraction of a degree matters.

THE HARD PART

Countries do not all have the same resources or responsibilities. Many lower-income countries say they need financial help to protect homes, farms and roads from climate damage and to build cleaner energy. Wealthier countries disagree about how much money should be provided and how it should be shared. There is also a practical challenge: leaders must turn promises into real projects after the conference ends.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

The conference agenda includes cutting methane — a powerful greenhouse gas — improving electricity networks, making cities more resilient to floods and heat, and increasing the use of electricity in daily life. Electricity can be much cleaner when it comes from wind, solar and other low-emission sources. Delegates are also discussing how to support countries already facing climate-related disasters.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

Cleaner energy can reduce pollution, create new kinds of jobs and make countries less dependent on imported fuels. But building power lines, storage batteries and renewable-energy projects takes money, materials, land and time. A fair plan must consider workers and communities that currently depend on fossil-fuel industries, too.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

If countries agree on a climate promise, what should happen next to make sure it becomes real? Who should check the progress, and how could ordinary people see whether it is working?

CANADA · QUEBEC · WILDLIFE

A surprise baby right whale is spotted near Anticosti Island

WHAT HAPPENED

Researchers identified a North Atlantic right whale calf in photographs taken off Anticosti Island in Quebec in September. The young whale was swimming with its mother, a 38-year-old whale known as Silt. Scientists had not recorded this calf before. Its discovery brings the latest count for the birthing season to 24 calves — the strongest season since 2009, according to researchers cited by The Associated Press.

WHY IT MATTERS

North Atlantic right whales are among the most endangered large whales. Researchers estimate that only about 380 remain. A new calf is encouraging because the population needs more young whales to survive and grow. Anticosti Island is in the Gulf of St. Lawrence, where whales may feed during warmer months.

THE HARD PART

Being born is only the first step. Right whales can be injured or killed when ships hit them or when they become tangled in fishing ropes. As ocean conditions change, whales may travel into places where protections are weaker or where boats and fishing gear are common. A good birth season does not guarantee that every calf will reach adulthood.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Scientists photograph and identify individual whales, track where they travel and study their health. Authorities can reduce risks through speed limits for ships, temporary fishing restrictions and safer fishing-gear designs. These measures are most useful when they are based on up-to-date information about where whales are swimming.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

Protecting whales can mean changing where and when ships travel or fishing takes place. Those changes can make work harder for fishers and businesses, but they can also prevent deaths and help keep ocean ecosystems healthy. The challenge is to protect wildlife while treating coastal communities fairly.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

💰 Economy & Business

Why clean energy needs more than solar panels and wind turbines.

ENERGY · INVESTMENT · EVERYDAY LIFE

Why renewable energy needs stronger electricity highways

WHAT HAPPENED

A report from the Associated Press on the opening of COP31 highlights a problem that is easy to overlook: some electricity networks cannot move all the power that wind and solar farms are ready to produce. When power lines are too limited, operators sometimes have to reduce production — even when the sun is shining or the wind is blowing. The International Energy Agency says annual investment in electricity grids needs to rise by about 50% from roughly US$400 billion to meet demand through 2030.

WHY IT MATTERS

Imagine a farm producing lots of fresh apples, but the road to the city is too narrow for the trucks. Some apples cannot reach the people who want them. Electricity has a similar problem. Solar panels and wind turbines generate power, but wires, transformers and storage systems must carry it to homes, schools, hospitals and factories. New demand from electric vehicles, heating and data centres makes the network even more important.

THE HARD PART

Power lines can take years to plan and build. They cross land owned by different people and communities, and they must be designed to work safely in storms, heat and winter weather. Batteries can store electricity for later, but large storage systems also cost money and need materials. A grid must balance supply and demand every moment.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Engineers and energy planners are expanding transmission lines, connecting regions, upgrading older equipment and adding batteries. Better links between places can move electricity from where it is generated to where it is needed. Smart systems can also help homes and businesses use more power when clean electricity is plentiful.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

A stronger grid can reduce wasted renewable electricity and make power supplies more reliable. But building it can affect landscapes and wildlife, and the cost may appear on electricity bills or public budgets. Planners must compare those costs with the cost of relying on polluting fuels or facing power shortages.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

If your community wanted more solar and wind power, what would you ask first: where the electricity would be generated, how it would travel, how it would be stored, or who would pay for the upgrades?

🤖 Technology & Science

Satellites and rockets help scientists understand changes high above Earth.

NASA · EARTH SCIENCE · ATMOSPHERE

NASA says the Antarctic ozone hole reached its 2026 maximum

WHAT HAPPENED

NASA reported on November 9 that the Antarctic ozone hole reached its largest size for 2026. It ranked seventh largest in the 48 years of satellite monitoring, although it was not as deep as some other large ozone holes. Scientists use satellites and other instruments to measure how much ozone is present high above Antarctica.

WHY IT MATTERS

The ozone layer is a region of the atmosphere that absorbs much of the Sun’s harmful ultraviolet radiation. Too much ultraviolet exposure can damage skin and eyes and harm plants and tiny ocean organisms. The ozone hole is a seasonal thinning of this protective layer, not a literal empty hole in the sky.

THE HARD PART

Ozone levels change with temperature, sunlight and chemicals in the atmosphere. The Antarctic ozone hole usually grows during the Southern Hemisphere’s spring, then shrinks as conditions change. One year’s measurement does not tell the whole story, so scientists compare many years of data.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Countries agreed in the 1987 Montreal Protocol to phase out many ozone-damaging chemicals once used in refrigerators, spray cans and other products. Scientists continue to measure the atmosphere to see whether the protective ozone layer is recovering and to spot unexpected changes.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

The recovery of the ozone layer is an example of countries working together on an environmental problem. But the recovery takes decades, and scientists must keep checking the data. It is a reminder that environmental rules can work — and that progress needs patience and monitoring.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

What made it possible for scientists to notice a change in the ozone layer over many decades? Why is it important to keep measuring something even after a rule has been introduced?

🎨 Creativity & Inspiration

Young people are using teamwork, coding and imagination to tackle real-world challenges.

SWEDEN · ROBOTICS · BIODIVERSITY

More than 350 young inventors build robots to help nature

WHAT HAPPENED

On November 9, more than 350 children and teenagers gathered at Sweden’s National Museum of Science and Technology in Stockholm for a regional final of FIRST LEGO League. This year’s challenge, called BIOGLOW, asks teams to explore how technology might help protect biodiversity — the variety of living things in an ecosystem. Participants build and program LEGO robots, and they also present ideas for solving environmental problems.

WHY IT MATTERS

Robots can help people collect information, inspect difficult-to-reach places and complete repetitive tasks. But a robot is only useful when someone has identified a real problem and designed a sensible solution. The competition encourages students to combine science, engineering, creativity and teamwork instead of treating them as separate subjects.

THE HARD PART

A robot may work perfectly in a classroom and fail on uneven ground or in rain. A nature-protection idea also needs evidence: does it actually help a species or habitat, and could it accidentally cause a new problem? Young inventors have to test, improve and explain their designs.

WHAT PEOPLE ARE TRYING

Teams spend weeks researching a problem, building prototypes, programming robots and testing different approaches. They share their work with judges and other students. The process rewards learning and improvement, not just having the fastest robot.

GOOD AND BAD / TRADE-OFFS

Robotics can make science exciting and help students learn practical skills. Equipment can be expensive, though, and not every school has the same access to kits, mentors or workshop space. Making competitions welcoming to more students is part of the challenge.

🤔 THINK ABOUT IT

Design a small robot or device that could help a local park, river or garden. What would it measure or do? How would you test whether it really helped?

A STUDENT IDEA WITH A PURPOSE

A 13-year-old experiments with plastic made from plants

In Montana, student Rye Rost won a national award for experimenting with bioplastic — plastic made from renewable materials. She tried using bananas, then changed her method and tested plantains. Her project shows that experiments often involve failed attempts and adjustments before a better result appears. Plant-based plastic is not automatically harmless: researchers still need to test how strong it is, how it breaks down and what resources are needed to make it. 🐾 Nature & Planet

A CLOSER LOOK

Why a baby whale is a reason for hope — but not a reason to stop protecting whales

The newly identified right-whale calf near Quebec is encouraging news, especially for a species with such a small population. But conservation is not finished when an animal is born. Scientists need to understand where whales travel, what dangers they face and whether protective measures reduce injuries. The lesson: a hopeful sign is important, but long-term evidence tells us whether a population is truly recovering.

One useful way to help wildlife is to notice the living things around you. Record birds in a local park, learn which plants attract pollinators, or join a supervised community clean-up. Small observations can become useful information when they are collected carefully and shared responsibly.
